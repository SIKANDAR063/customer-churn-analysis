1. The overall churn rate is 22%, indicating a significant level of customer attrition.

2. Small and large companies exhibit the highest churn rates (~23–24%), whereas enterprise customers are more stable (~19%), likely due to long-term commitments.

3. Customers acquired through events have the highest churn (~26%), suggesting low-intent or low-quality acquisition channels.

4. Partner-based acquisition shows the lowest churn (~15%), indicating higher customer trust and reliability.

5. NPS alone is not a strong predictor of churn, as even high NPS customers are observed to churn.

6. This highlights the need to combine satisfaction metrics with behavioral data for accurate churn prediction.

7. Customers with low activity levels (fewer interactions in MongoDB logs) demonstrate a higher probability of churn.

8. Engagement (user activity) is a stronger and more reliable predictor of retention compared to NPS alone.

9. Combining structured data (SQL) with behavioral data (MongoDB) provides a more comprehensive and accurate understanding of churn patterns.
